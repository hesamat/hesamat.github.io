Teaching tools

CoursebookMD

Teaching tools · JavaScript · ongoing · GitHub

I write my course material as connected Markdown chapters, and I wanted the lecture and the student-facing site to come from the same document instead of diverging into slides and handouts. CoursebookMD is that tool: a scroll-and-spotlight presentation mode, syntax highlighting, math and diagrams, an auto-generated index of marked terms, and a static export for students.

Its sibling, SlideMD, applies the same idea to traditional decks — Markdown in, dual-window presenter view and clean PDF out, with PPTX import for everything the world insists on sending as PowerPoint.

Earlier research

HappyFeet: Embodiments for Joint Remote Dancing

Research · Graphics Interface 2016 · Code · MiddleMan relay

HappyFeet let dance partners in different rooms share a floor: 3D renderings of their feet, tracked and projected into one virtual space, making timing and placement visible. In a lab study, the feet embodiment helped people understand moves, synchronize their steps, and improvise together. I built the client in C#/WPF and wrote MiddleMan, a Node.js relay that carried the time-series stream between the two sides — with recording and playback, because lab studies always need playback.

Haptics in Remote Collaborative Exercise

Research · CHI 2014

Group exercise motivates people, but mobility and transportation keep many seniors at home. This prototype let a remote partner's presence be felt: a wrist-worn haptic band rendered three vibration metaphors — constant push/pull, corrective feedback, and notification — standing in for the physical language of exercising together. The contribution was a set of design lessons for haptic feedback in remote group exercise.

Automatic Calibration of an Air Hockey Robot

Research · ICRoM 2013

My University of Tehran master's work, in collaboration with a mechanical engineer who built the robot while I worked on the brains: a learning algorithm that lets an air-hockey robot adapt to a new table — its particular friction and bounce — from a handful of shots. In simulation it calibrated itself in about seventeen shots and planned shots to within about one percent error, no high-speed camera required. Matlab, C++, and OpenCV.
